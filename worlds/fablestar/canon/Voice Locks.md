# Voice Locks — Fablestar Expanse

Per [[Rules]] R-023, VO and dialogue are generated by H3. A voice is held by a **byte-identical
identity phrase** placed **outside** `<d>`, carrying five fields: character type, age, pitch,
timbre, speaking rate. Only the quoted line inside `<d>` changes between beats.

> [!important] **Copy verbatim. Never paraphrase, never trim, never tune one for a particular line.**
> A voice that is re-described per clip is a voice that drifts per clip.

## The lock is the phrase, not the ID

`(Sx)` is assigned **per clip**, by the order of actual vocal events in that clip — it is not a
per-series number. [[Dov]] is `(S1)` in B1c and B1d because he speaks first there; [[Kess]] is
`(S1)` in B1a, B1b and B1e. The identity phrase is what holds the voice across clips. The ID is
bookkeeping.

---

## KESS

```
a thin reedy young male voice in his early twenties, high-pitched and slightly nasal, the timbre light and unsupported with a faint crack at the top of his range, speaking quickly and running his words together
```

Rattles, over-explains, narrates his own work because silence frightens him. The crack at the top of
his range is what makes him twenty-two rather than thirty.

## MIRA

```
a low worn woman's voice in her fifties, deep and level in pitch, the timbre dry and slightly roughened, speaking slowly and evenly with flat unvaried stress and no rise at the end of a sentence
```

**"No rise at the end of a sentence" is the load-bearing clause.** She asks questions without asking
them — *"What do you tell her?"* lands flat, not curious. Says the hard thing once and does not
repeat it.

## DOV

```
a heavy male voice in his mid-forties, low and thick in pitch, the timbre broad and gravelly and a little muffled as though produced without much effort, speaking slowly and lazily with long gaps between phrases
```

**"Without much effort" is the character.** He complains as a hobby, not a grievance. Everything he
says is undersold, including the line about his wife.

## LAN — ⚠️ NOT YET WRITTEN

Per the OS, the old byte-identical AUDIO-block lock has **not** been ported to the new `(Sx)`
identity-phrase format, and that port must happen before any clip in which he speaks. He does not
speak until **B6**, so this is not blocking B1 — but it blocks B6 and every beat after it.

Constraints when it is written: [[Lan]] is rough, dry, worn-in, casually profane, wrecked and
amused in B6 after ninety-one hours under. The [[Rules]] R-023 narration form — *says in an
off-screen voiceover* plus the closed-lips clause — applies only to narration beats, of which the
cold open has none.

## Beat 12 note, carried forward

The old guidance that **exactly one voice appears per clip** is retired. B1 alone runs two speakers
in every clip. The surviving rule is narrower: **no second speaker ID appears in a beat that is
built around a single voice**, and speaker count is decided per clip by what the scene actually
contains.
